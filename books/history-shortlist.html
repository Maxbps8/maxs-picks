Books

A history shortlist — books that read like a story, and a few that go deeper

This is a starter list for readers who want history, including narrative history you can actually finish in the evening. It sits in the general Books section of Max's Picks, not only in the Christian-books vertical.

Narrative history (start here if you want a story)

  • 1776 — David McCullough. A readable account of one year of the American Revolution. A solid first book if you like history without academic apparatus. Bookshop.org · Amazon · Audible · Libro.fm
  • The Splendid and the Vile — Erik Larson. Churchill’s household and London during the Blitz, told scene by scene. Good audio, too, if you commute. Bookshop.org · Amazon · Audible · Libro.fm
  • Team of Rivals — Doris Kearns Goodwin. Lincoln and his cabinet. Long, and worth it if you like political history with people in it. Bookshop.org · Amazon · Audible · Libro.fm
  • The Guns of August — Barbara Tuchman. The opening of World War I, written with pace. Still a standard starting point for that war. Bookshop.org · Amazon · Audible · Libro.fm
  • The Devil in the White City — Erik Larson. The 1893 Chicago World’s Fair and the crimes of H.H. Holmes. History with a true-crime strand; if you want the crime side first, see the true-crime list on this site. Bookshop.org · Amazon · Audible · Libro.fm
  • Killers of the Flower Moon — David Grann. The murders of Osage people in 1920s Oklahoma, and the early FBI. Reported history that also belongs next to the true-crime shelf. Bookshop.org · Amazon · Audible · Libro.fm

A step deeper

  • Battle Cry of Freedom — James M. McPherson. A one-volume Civil War history that is still widely used as a starting “serious” book. It is long. Do not wrap it as a casual paperback unless you know the reader wants that depth. Bookshop.org · Amazon · Audible · Libro.fm
  • The Warmth of Other Suns — Isabel Wilkerson. The Great Migration, told through the lives of people who made it. History with a clear human through-line. Bookshop.org · Amazon · Audible · Libro.fm
  • Say Nothing — Patrick Radden Keefe. Modern reporting on the Troubles in Northern Ireland. Gripping, and not gentle. Keefe’s 2026 book London Falling is on the year-end gift shortlist if you want something newer. Bookshop.org · Amazon · Audible · Libro.fm

How I would use this list

If you want one book: McCullough or Larson. If you want a long winter book: Goodwin or McPherson. If you like reported narrative that is closer to the present: Grann or Keefe.

Honest limits

This is a starter shortlist of widely known titles, not a claim that I have reread every page this year, and not a ranked “best of all time.” If Max has not read a book and is not comfortable recommending it under his name, it should be removed or relabeled before this page goes live.
