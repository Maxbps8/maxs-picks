Books

A business shortlist — useful books, not guru posters

How companies actually work

  • The Innovator’s Dilemma — Clayton M. Christensen. The classic argument about why good companies miss the next thing. Drier than a memoir, and still the book a lot of later business writing is riffing on. Bookshop.org · Amazon · Audible · Libro.fm
  • Good to Great — Jim Collins. Influential, readable, and later criticized because some of the “great” companies did not stay that way. Worth reading as a set of ideas about discipline and hiring, not as a guaranteed formula. I will not pretend the debate does not exist. Bookshop.org · Amazon · Audible · Libro.fm
  • The Hard Thing About Hard Things — Ben Horowitz. A blunt book about running a company when things are going badly. More useful than a victory lap. Bookshop.org · Amazon · Audible · Libro.fm
  • The Lean Startup — Eric Ries. The popular book on testing a product in small loops instead of building in a cave. Some of the language is now overused; the core habit is still sound. Bookshop.org · Amazon · Audible · Libro.fm

Money, judgment, and deals

  • The Intelligent Investor — Benjamin Graham (look for a recent edition with Jason Zweig’s commentary unless you have a reason to want an older text). The value-investing foundation. Not a hot-stock book, and not light. Bookshop.org · Amazon · Audible · Libro.fm
  • The Essays of Warren Buffett — edited by Lawrence Cunningham, if you want Buffett’s own letters arranged by theme rather than a biography. Confirm the edition year. Bookshop.org · Amazon
  • Never Split the Difference — Chris Voss. A negotiation book from a former FBI negotiator. Practical, a little slick in places, and more useful than most “win the room” business titles. Bookshop.org · Amazon · Audible · Libro.fm

Company stories

  • Shoe Dog — Phil Knight. The founding story of Nike, written as a memoir. One of the better business stories, even if you do not care about sneakers. It listens well. Bookshop.org · Amazon · Audible · Libro.fm

How I would use this list

If you want a story: Shoe Dog. If you want a construction-site book about management: Horowitz. If you want investing: Graham, slowly. If someone is pushing Good to Great as a complete operating system, read it — and then read the caveats.

Honest limits

This is a starter shortlist of widely known titles, not a claim that I have reread every page this year, and not a ranked “best of all time.” If Max has not read a book and is not comfortable recommending it under his name, it should be removed or relabeled before this page goes live.

Note: This is a book list, not investment advice. I am not recommending that you buy or sell any security.
