Winter tires and wheels for a Model S Plaid — a 19-inch set, not snow tires on the 21s

Northern Kentucky and Cincinnati winters are messy more often than they are dramatic: slush, salt, and the occasional ice morning. A Plaid on summer or performance all-seasons is not at its best in that. The high-leverage decision is a dedicated winter tire, preferably on its own set of wheels, so you are not mounting and dismounting twice a year and so the 21-inch summer wheels stay off the salt.

This is a companion to the Kentucky and Ohio winter kit and the wheel and tire care page. Those are general. This one is about a Model S Plaid.

Why I’m looking at 19-inch winter wheels

I would rather run a winter tire on a 19-inch wheel than stretch a snow tire onto the 21-inch wheels. The taller sidewall is more forgiving on potholes and broken freeway ramps, the tires usually cost less in that size, and the summer set can stay clean in the garage. If you want to keep the 21-inch look all winter, that is a style choice; it is not the setup I’m choosing.

Tesla’s own 19-inch winter package, when I looked at the Shop page on November 27, 2026, uses staggered Pirelli P Zero Winter tires: 255/45R19 in front and 285/40R19 in the rear, on 19-inch Tempest wheels. That is the size set I’m treating as the starting point. Some owners run 255/45R19 on all four corners so the tires can be rotated; if you do that, confirm with the installer and with Tesla or the tire maker that it is acceptable for your car. I will not invent a handling comparison I have not driven.

Three ways to buy the same idea

1. Tesla’s own 19-inch winter package

The official package is the cleanest path if it is in stock and if your car is actually eligible. When I checked, Tesla listed a 2021–2025 Model S 19-inch Tempest wheel and winter tire package with Pirelli P Zero Winters in the sizes above, plus sensors, and with shipping to a Tesla Service Center and installation included. The same page described it as compatible with 2021–2025 Model S vehicles with 19-inch Tempest wheels, and it was not a simple “add to cart” item. I drive a 2026. I would not order that package until Tesla, or Cincinnati service, confirms in writing that it fits a 2026 Plaid.

Tesla also lists a 21-inch Arachnid winter package. That is the “keep the 21s” path, not the one I’m choosing. Confirm current stock and year fitment on the Shop page; I am not treating a listed package as a promise that it is orderable this week.

  • Tesla 21-inch Arachnid winter package (only if you want to stay on 21s): Model S 21-inch Arachnid wheel and winter tire package

2. Tires from a retailer, mounted at a shop you trust

If the Tesla package does not apply to your year, or you would rather choose the wheel yourself, the tire is still the P Zero Winter in the sizes that match the wheel. Shop a current listing at Tire Rack for the Pirelli P Zero Winter, or a local shop that can order that size, and pay the installer separately for mounting, balancing, and a TPMS-safe setup. I looked at Tire Discounters (a Cincinnati-founded, family-owned chain), Xpress Pro in Edgewood, and a few other Northern Kentucky shops. I have not had this set mounted yet, so I am not anointing a “best shop.” Call, ask whether they will lift a Plaid at Tesla’s jack points, and ask whether they can get the staggered sizes.

  • Pirelli P Zero Winter at Tire Rack: P Zero Winter on Tire Rack
  • Pirelli’s own winter-tire page: Pirelli P Zero Winter

3. An aftermarket 19-inch wheel, if you do not want Tempest

I also compared forged 19-inch wheels aimed at the Plaid, including the Unplugged Performance UP-05 and T Sportline sets such as the TSSF 19-inch package and the TS114 19-inch package. Some of those can be bought as wheels only or as a mounted winter package. Confirm the exact width, offset, and load rating, and confirm brake clearance — especially if your car has a Track Pack or carbon-ceramic brakes, which some 19-inch wheels may not clear. I have not bought a set, and I will not invent a ride-quality ranking.

What I would not do

  • Put winter tires on the 21-inch summer wheels unless you have a clear reason and a shop that will dismount them twice a year without chewing up the wheels.
  • Guess a size off a forum post. Use the door sticker, the official package, or a written confirmation from the seller.
  • Ignore storage. A second set of wheels needs a dry place. If you do not have one, ask the shop about seasonal storage before you buy.
  • Treat a new tire as a substitute for a careful driveway on an ice morning. Winter tires help. They do not repeal physics.

Straight recommendation

For a Model S Plaid I actually drive in Kentucky and Ohio winters, I’m choosing a 19-inch wheel with Pirelli P Zero Winters in the factory staggered sizes, and I’m leaving the 21-inch wheels in the garage until spring. Start with Tesla’s official 19-inch package if they will sell and install it for your year. If they will not, buy the same tire from a retailer and have a shop that knows how to lift a Tesla mount it on a 19-inch wheel that clears your brakes. Confirm every size and the current price on the live page. For pressure, salt, and cleaning after you have the set, use the wheel and tire care page.
