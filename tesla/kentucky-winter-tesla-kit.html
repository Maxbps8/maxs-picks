A practical winter kit for a Tesla in Kentucky or Ohio

Northern Kentucky winters are not Minnesota winters. They are messy: freeze-thaw, road salt, wet snow, and the occasional ice morning. A Tesla handles that like any other car if you do the ordinary work — tires, washer fluid, and a way to keep slush out of the cabin — and if you understand that cold weather reduces range and slows charging. That last part is physics, not a defect.

This kit is a short list. It is not a survivalist loadout, and it is not a claim that any accessory will “fix” winter range.

What I would actually put in the car

Use this as a packing list. Buy only what you do not already have.

1. All-weather floor liners and a cargo liner

Salt and slush will wreck a carpet. See the longer mat guide for fitment rules.

  • Vehicle-specific liners: WeatherTech Tesla FloorLiners or Tesla shop floor mats
  • Amazon fallback: all-weather Tesla floor mats on Amazon

In November 2026 I ordered all-weather interior liners from the Tesla Shop, in the same order as an outlet adapter and an air compressor and tire-repair kit. Confirm the current kit, year fitment, and whether it has arrived in a condition you can inspect before you copy that order. The longer fitment rules are still in the Model 3 and Model Y mat guide; if you drive a Model S, use the Shop listing for your exact year, not a 3/Y kit.

2. A window and mirror plan

You want a proper ice scraper and a small bottle of washer fluid rated for winter temperatures. You do not need a branded “Tesla” scraper. A sturdy scraper that will not gouge glass is enough.

  • Ice scraper / snow brush: ice scraper on Amazon
  • Washer fluid (winter-rated — read the freeze point on the jug): buy locally if that is cheaper; otherwise winter washer fluid on Amazon

3. Tires, not gadgets

If you still have worn all-seasons, a $30 accessory will not help you on a slushy I-75 on-ramp. The high-leverage winter decision is tire tread and, if you regularly drive in snow, a dedicated winter or a well-rated all-weather tire. Get the size from the door sticker, then shop a tire retailer or a current test (Consumer Reports, Tire Rack, or similar) and confirm pricing there.

If you drive a Model S Plaid, I wrote a separate page for the setup I’m choosing: a 19-inch winter wheel with Pirelli P Zero Winters, not snow tires stretched onto the 21-inch summer wheels. That comparison, including Tesla’s own package and aftermarket wheels, is here: winter tires and wheels for a Model S Plaid. I have not bought that set yet.

Wheel-care products, if you need them: Tesla wheel and tire care at T Sportline

4. Cabin comfort: a reusable cloth and a small trash bag

Wet gloves and a soaked umbrella will drip into the same footwell you just protected. A dedicated towel and a bag for wet gear is unglamorous and useful.

  • Microfiber towels: absorbent towels on Amazon

5. Charging in the cold

Home Level 2 charging is more comfortable in winter because the car can precondition and you start the day with a known charge. See the charger guide rather than doubling that article here.

  • Confirmed Level 2 unit or adapter: Lectron Tesla / NACS chargers or Level 2 EV charger on Amazon

If you rely on Superchargers in deep cold, budget extra time. The car and the charger will tell you the speed. I will not invent a percentage range loss for your commute.

6. A small emergency kit that is not theater

For a Cincinnati / NKY commute, a reasonable kit is: a charged phone cable, a compact first-aid pouch, a flashlight, a reflective triangle or vest, and a pair of gloves you can actually work in. A foldable shovel lives in the trunk if you park on a slope or a rural spur. You probably do not need a week of freeze-dried food for a 20-minute drive to work. The Tesla Shop compressor and tire-repair kit I ordered belongs in this category too — a way to add air, not a substitute for a winter tire. Confirm what the current Shop bundle actually includes.

  • LED flashlight: flashlight on Amazon
  • First-aid pouch: car first-aid kit on Amazon
  • Compact shovel (only if you will actually keep it in the car): compact snow shovel on Amazon

Things I would skip

  • Mystery “range extender” gadgets and most plug-in winter gizmos that promise to restore summer range. They don’t override cold-weather battery chemistry.
  • Unlabeled no-name charging adapters. Winter is a bad time to discover a poor connector.
  • Cosmetic spoilers and the like. They are not a winter kit.

A short preconditioning reminder

Tesla’s own owner materials describe preconditioning the cabin and, when you navigate to a Supercharger, preparing the battery. Use the car’s app and owner documentation for the current steps. I am not reprinting menu paths that may change with a software update.

Straight recommendation

Spend money first on tires and liners, then on a scraper and winter washer fluid, then on charging convenience if you still need it. Everything else is optional.
