Aftermarket wheels for a Model S Plaid — BBS, 21-inch street sets, and what has to fit

I drive a 2026 Model S Plaid on 21-inch wheels. I have compared aftermarket 21-inch options, including BBS-related packages. I have not bought a set yet, and I am not marking this as a road test. Confirm year, brake package, load rating, and offset on the seller’s page and with the installer before you order.

This is the street-wheel companion to the 19-inch winter tire and wheel guide. That page is about getting through Kentucky slush. This one is about replacing or stepping off the factory 21s in warm weather. I would still keep a dedicated winter set, not mount snow tires on expensive 21-inch wheels. The wheel and tire care page covers pressure and salt.

Start with Tesla’s own numbers

Tesla’s Model S owner’s manual, checked November 27, 2026, lists these factory sizes and offsets:

  • 19-inch: front 19 × 9.5J, offset 40 mm; rear 19 × 10.5J, offset 45 mm. Factory all-season sizes in the U.S. are 255/45R19 front and 285/40R19 rear.
  • 21-inch: front 21 × 9.5J, offset 40 mm; rear 21 × 10.5J, offset 45 mm. Factory U.S. sizes are 265/35R21 (or 265/35ZR21 summer) front and 295/30R21 (or 295/30ZR21 summer) rear.
  • Lug-nut torque: 129 lb-ft (175 Nm), 21 mm socket.

Those figures are on Tesla’s Wheels and Tires owner’s manual page. Aftermarket wheels should be sold as a Tesla-specific 2021-and-newer Model S / Plaid package, not as a “close enough” 5-lug wheel from another car. The usual pattern those specialists publish is a 5×120 bolt pattern and a 64.1 mm center bore. I want a published load rating suited to a heavy, high-torque car, and I want the seller to say the wheel clears Plaid brakes.

BBS

I looked at BBS because the name means something on a street car, and because there is a Tesla-specific collaboration rather than a generic BBS catalog wheel. Unplugged Performance’s UP × BBS UP-01 write-up describes a 21-inch package engineered and manufactured by BBS in Germany for Model S and Model X, designed around Tesla’s standard 21-inch tire sizes, and said to work with original-equipment TPMS sensors and lug nuts. That is their product claim. I have not had a set on the car.

Unplugged also keeps a Model S / Plaid wheel and tire guide with factory sizes and their own fitments. Use that, and the live product page, not a screenshot from last year.

21-inch street sets, including a style I searched as “Absolute Mythos”

I also looked at other 21-inch street wheels, including a design I had searched under the name Absolute Mythos. I could not confirm a single official product page I trust for that exact name, so I am not going to link a random listing and pretend it is a verified fit. If you chase a style you saw in a photo, take the photo to a Tesla-specific wheel shop and make them match bolt pattern, offset, center bore, load rating, and brake clearance.

Two shops that do publish 2021–2026 Model S Plaid 21-inch packages, including TPMS, are Unplugged Performance and T Sportline’s Model S 2021–2026 wheel collection. T Sportline states that its 21-inch flow-forged TSR, TSF, and TSS wheels are not compatible with the Tesla Track Package / carbon-ceramic brakes, and that those cars should use a fully forged 21-inch wheel. If you have that brake package, start there. I do not, and I will not guess around it.

Their 21-inch packages are typically staggered 21×9.5 front and 21×10.5 rear, often at a slightly lower offset than Tesla’s +40 / +45. “Slightly lower offset” is still a fitment decision. Have the installer test-fit before the tires are mounted if the shop is not selling a documented bolt-on package.

TPMS

The car needs working tire-pressure sensors. Tesla-specific packages from the shops above are sold with factory-spec Tesla TPMS, already in the wheels, or they say the wheels accept the factory sensors. That is the path I would take. A generic sensor that the car will not read is a false economy. Have the shop program and road-check the sensors after install. I will not invent a sensor part number or a radio frequency.

A note on HW4, cameras, and brakes

A 2026 Model S uses Tesla’s newer camera-based Autopilot hardware, often called HW4. Some later cars also have a front bumper camera. I have not measured whether a particular aftermarket wheel sits in a camera’s view, and I will not invent a clearance number. Before you buy a deep-concave, unusually wide, or spacer-dependent wheel, ask the seller whether they have fitted that exact size on a 2021+ or 2026 Plaid, and have the installer check inner clearance to the brake caliper and that nothing obvious sits in a camera’s way.

Brake clearance is the note I would not skip. Plaid calipers are large. Track-package carbon ceramics are a different problem, as T Sportline already flags. If the listing does not say it clears your brakes, it is not a listing for this car.

Straight recommendation

If I buy a 21-inch street set, I will buy a Tesla-specific package that publishes fitment, load rating, TPMS, and brake clearance — the UP × BBS set is the BBS path I am looking at, and T Sportline or Unplugged’s own 21-inch packages are the fallback if I want a different face. I will not cheap out on a no-name wheel for a Plaid. For winter, I am still looking at a 19-inch set; that decision is on the winter tire guide.
