I drive a 2026 Model S Plaid. I wanted a detector that could be installed cleanly, without a power cord hanging down the dash, and I wanted an installer who would treat the car carefully. That is as far as this has gone: research, and a wish list, not a completed install I can review.

A detector does not make a speeding ticket less expensive, and it does not change the speed limit. Radar detectors are legal in most US states for a passenger car, and they are restricted or banned in some places (including, last I checked, Virginia and the District of Columbia). Confirm the current law where you drive, including any state you pass through. I am writing this from Kentucky, for a car I drive in Kentucky and Ohio. I am not a lawyer.

Power and mounting in a Model S

A cigarette-lighter cord is the easy path and the ugly path. The cleaner path is a hardwire kit that takes ignition power at the rearview mirror, so nothing hangs across the windshield.

The kit I added to the same wish list is the MirrorTap MTX-1010, also sold on Amazon. It is a short, RJ-11 power cord with a fused tap, made in the US by the same company behind BlendMount. MirrorTap’s own listing says it is compatible with Uniden R8 models. Confirm that on the live page for the exact detector you buy. I looked for a BlendMount made specifically for the Model S and did not find one I would order; a windshield mount plus this power kit is the combination I’m choosing instead.

Two Tesla-specific cautions, which an installer should verify rather than take from this paragraph:

  • Tesla windshields have a coating that can interfere with a radar detector except on an uncoated patch near the mirror. Ask the installer and the detector maker where the unit is supposed to sit. Do not stick it in the middle of the glass and hope.
  • Some Tesla circuits are not a simple 12-volt feed. The installer should confirm voltage and an ignition-switched source with a meter before anything is connected. MirrorTap’s own instructions are built around that check. If you are not comfortable with a meter, this is a shop job. I have not done this install, and I will not walk you through cutting into a harness.

A professional car-audio or accessory shop that has worked on Teslas is the path I would take. I have not hired one for this yet, so I am not naming a “best installer” in Northern Kentucky.

Things I would skip

  • A no-name detector with a confident Amazon title and no way to get firmware updates.
  • A long cord across the dash, unless you are testing the unit for a week before you commit to a hardwire.
  • Mounting on the coated part of the windshield, or anywhere that blocks the driver’s view.
  • Treating a detector as permission to drive faster than the road and the weather allow.

Straight recommendation

If I put a detector in the Plaid, it will be a current Uniden R8w, hardwired with a MirrorTap MTX-1010, mounted where the glass and the maker say it will actually work, by someone who will check the circuit first. Confirm the current model, compatibility, and price on the official pages. I will update this if I have the install done and can speak to the result, not only the shopping list.
