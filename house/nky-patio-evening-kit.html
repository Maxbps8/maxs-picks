A paver patio in this climate is usable more evenings than people think, if you solve three boring problems: a dip in the temperature after sunset, a little light that is not a bare bulb, and mosquitoes in the warm months. I bought a kit for those three jobs. The fire is a separate decision — that is the Breeo X24 vs X30 page, and I have not bought the pit yet.

A propane patio heater

I bought an EAST OAK 55,000 BTU propane patio heater with a round table and wheels in bronze. The official catalog is on EAST OAK’s patio heater collection. I wanted something I could roll out of the way, with a small table around the post, for a pad that also has to hold chairs. I will not invent a heat-radius chart or tell you it replaces a fire pit. It does not. Read the live manual for clearance to eaves, umbrellas, and the house, and use it only outdoors.

If that exact finish is gone, confirm you are still looking at a 55,000 BTU propane tower with a listed tip-over or fail-safe, wheels, and a current instruction sheet — or search the same heater on Amazon and then read the listing twice.

Solar lanterns, not a string of cheap fairy lights

I bought a 13.4-inch Tree of Life hanging solar lantern and a two-pack of GIGALUMI solar firework lights. They are decorative, not a floodlight. I wanted something I could hang or set out without running an outdoor outlet to every corner. Solar lanterns only work if they actually see sun; a deep, shaded porch will disappoint you, and I will not pretend otherwise.

I am not picking a “best lantern.” These are the ones I ordered. If you want a similar tree-style lantern, search Tree of Life solar lanterns on Amazon and check the height, whether it is listed as outdoor-rated, and the return policy.

A mosquito trap for the warm months

I bought a DynaTrap DT2030SR outdoor mosquito and flying-insect trap. The maker’s page is DynaTrap’s official site. The listing describes a UV-LED plus fan design and a one-acre class of coverage. That coverage number is the manufacturer’s claim for an open area, not a promise about a screened porch or a windy hillside in Villa Hills. I am not going to invent a bug count.

Place it away from where people sit, so it draws insects toward the trap and not toward the table. Read the current instructions for height, power, and how often to empty it. If you need a replacement, search the DT2030SR on Amazon and match the model number, not just the brand.

What I would not skip, and what this kit is not

  • A fire pit is not in this kit. If you want flame and a place for the wood, use the Breeo and firewood-rack guide and confirm size on a 12-to-14-foot pad.
  • A patio heater is not a blanket exemption from clearance rules. Keep it away from low roofs and fabric.
  • I would not treat a decorative solar lantern as path lighting for a dark side yard.
  • I would not run a mosquito trap and then sit directly next to it.

Straight recommendation

For evenings on a Northern Kentucky patio, the kit I actually bought is a roll-around propane heater, a couple of solar lanterns, and a DynaTrap. Add a fire pit only if you want one, and size that separately. Check today’s product pages for the current price and the current spec sheet. I will update this if a season of weather teaches me something those pages do not.
