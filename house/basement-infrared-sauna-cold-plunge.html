An indoor infrared sauna and cold plunge that actually fits a finished basement

I want a two-person infrared sauna and a cold plunge in a finished basement in Villa Hills, Kentucky. I first looked at putting them outside, on a deck or a porch. After I measured an interior room, I switched to an indoor plan. I have not ordered anything. If you are in the same spot — a low ceiling, a staircase, and a room that is not huge — the useful work is measuring, not collecting brochures.

This is not medical advice. If you have a heart condition, blood-pressure issues, are pregnant, or have any doubt about heat or cold therapy, talk to your doctor first. Start shorter than the marketing suggests, and get out if you feel lightheaded or unwell.

Measure the room before you fall for a cabin

Measure from the floor to the lowest thing above the spot where the sauna would sit: a joist, a duct, a pipe, a sprinkler, a light. Write down length, width, and that lowest height. Then add the clearance the maker asks for, not the clearance you wish they asked for.

The room I measured is about 7 ft 10 in by 9 ft 6 in, with a 7 ft 6 in (90-inch) ceiling and a concrete floor. There is no exterior door. That one number, 90 inches, ruled out a cabin that would otherwise have been in the mix.

What I’m choosing, and why

I want infrared, not a traditional wood-fired or high-temperature electric sauna. I want it to fit two people. And I want it indoors, because the deck would need a serious look at load, and a Kentucky winter is a hard place to discover that a cabin does not like being left out.

After comparing Clearlight, Plunge, and Kohler, I narrowed it to Clearlight or Plunge unless something else clearly beat them on fit. Kohler was in a different price world. Plunge makes an infrared cabin now; the official specs are why it dropped out of my room, not a brand preference.

The sauna: Clearlight Sanctuary 2

The Clearlight Sanctuary 2 is a two-person full-spectrum infrared cabin. On Clearlight’s product page, as of November 27, 2026, the exterior is 51 3/4 inches wide by 47 3/4 inches deep by 77 inches tall, and the cabin is listed at 450 lb. They recommend leaving 4 inches around it in an enclosed space. It is listed as plugging into a 120-volt, 20-amp outlet (NEMA 5-20P). Confirm those figures on the live page; I am not installing it for you.

In a 90-inch room, 77 inches of cabin height leaves some air above the roof. That is why this is the cabin I’m choosing for this room. If your ceiling is lower than that, look at a shorter model and confirm the current height and clearance on the maker’s page before you pay a deposit. Clearlight also sells the cabin in basswood or mahogany; I have not picked a wood yet.

The plunge: Nordic Wave Viking XL

A long, lie-down tub is a poor fit next to a two-person cabin in a room this size, and a heavy assembled tub is a bad thing to wrestle down basement stairs. The upright tub I’m choosing to put beside the Sanctuary 2 is the Nordic Wave Viking XL. It is not a Clearlight product. I asked that question because the names get blurred in sales pages.

Nordic Wave’s product page, as of November 27, 2026, lists the Viking XL as a two-person upright tub: 43 inches wide by 33 inches deep by 48 inches tall on the outside, 160 gallons at maximum fill, 75 lb empty and 1,118 lb with water, cooling to 35°F and heating to 104°F. That empty weight is a much more realistic basement-stairs problem than a several-hundred-pound assembled lie-down tub. Confirm the live specs, what is included (chiller, lid, steps, filters), and whether it is rated for indoor use in a finished room.

What does not fit, or does not fit well, in this room

Plunge’s infrared cabin

Plunge’s Infrared Sauna (and the 2-person Infrared Sauna Pro) is listed at 53 inches wide by 51.75 inches deep by 80.5 inches tall. Plunge’s own installation guide also asks for 12 inches of clearance above the highest point of the sauna, plus 26.5 inches in front of the door and 6 inches at the sides and rear. Eighty and a half inches of cabin plus twelve inches above it is 92.5 inches. My ceiling is 90 inches. That is the end of that comparison for this room. If your ceiling is taller, it is still a fair infrared cabin to look at; I am not ranking heat quality I have not sat in.

Clearlight’s own lie-down plunge

Clearlight sells a long cold plunge that can be bundled with the sauna. On the current product page it is a roughly 78-by-30-inch tub, and it arrives assembled at a weight I would not want to take down my stairs. In this room, next to the Sanctuary 2, it also leaves very little space to walk. I had it priced as a second option so I could compare. For this layout, the upright Viking XL is the better fit. If you have a longer wall and an easier path into the room, look at the official page and measure both ways.

The unglamorous parts cost money too

The cabin and the tub are not the whole bill. Plan for a licensed electrician, and ask whether your room needs two dedicated circuits and a permit or inspection. I am not quoting a labor number I have not been given in writing by a local electrician.

A basement already wants help with moisture. A cold tub and a sauna will add more. A dehumidifier, an exhaust path, and rubber mats around the wet area are part of the setup, not extras to remember in March. I have not picked a specific dehumidifier or mat yet, so I am not going to pretend I have a favorite SKU.

Ask the plunge maker what water care and filters the tub actually needs, and what the yearly supplies look like, before you treat the sale price as the whole cost.

Things I would skip

  • Ordering a cabin from a photo without measuring to the lowest overhead obstruction.
  • Putting a several-hundred-pound assembled tub in a basement without a plan for the stairs and the filled weight on the floor.
  • Treating a single “hot and cold” tub as a substitute for a sauna if what you want is a short walk from heat into cold. A tub that changes temperature slowly is not a contrast circuit.
  • Health claims that sound like a clinic. Sweat and cold can feel good. They are not a diagnosis or a cure.

Straight recommendation

If you have a finished basement with about eight feet of ceiling or less, start with a cabin whose official height, plus the maker’s required clearance, actually fits under the lowest pipe. For my room, that is the Clearlight Sanctuary 2 and an upright Nordic Wave Viking XL, not Plunge’s taller infrared cabin and not a long assembled tub I would have to get down the stairs. Confirm every dimension, circuit, and current price on the official pages, and hire an electrician before you take a delivery date. I will update this if I buy the pair and learn something I cannot see on a spec sheet.
