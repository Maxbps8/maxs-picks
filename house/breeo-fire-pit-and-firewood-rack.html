A Breeo fire pit for a paver patio — X24 vs X30, plus a place to store the wood

This is what I’m comparing for a paver pad in my yard. Prices, sizes, finishes, shipping, and return rules change — confirm them on the seller’s page before you order.

I have a paver pad in the yard, roughly 12 to 14 feet across, away from the house and the deck, already used as a fire-pit foundation. I’m shopping for a Breeo X Series pit to go on it, and for a rack that will keep the wood off the ground. I asked whether anyone near Villa Hills had a Breeo in stock; I could not confirm shelf stock online, so a current call or a ship-to-home order is the practical path. I have not placed that order.

This is a sizing guide, not a lab test of smoke, and not a claim that any wood-burning pit is truly “smokeless” in every wind. Read the current product page and the user guide. Keep the pit on a surface that can take the heat, and keep it away from the house, the deck, and low branches.

X24 or X30 on a 12-to-14-foot pad

Breeo’s X Series is the line I’m looking at: a US-made, double-wall pit sold in several diameters. On Breeo’s product page as of November 27, 2026, the two sizes that make sense for this pad are:

  • X24: 27.5-inch outside diameter, 23.5-inch inside diameter, 14.75 inches tall, 62 lb. Recommended wood length 16 to 18 inches.
  • X30: 34.5-inch outside diameter, 29.5-inch inside diameter, 14.75 inches tall, 94 lb. Recommended wood length up to 24 inches.

The X19 is a small-yard size. The X42 is 46 inches across and would eat more of a 12-to-14-foot pad than I want to give it, once you add chairs and a walkway. Both the X24 and the X30 physically fit; the decision is how you sit around it and how long a log you want to burn.

If you mostly sit two to four people and you are fine splitting wood down to the mid-teens of inches, the X24 is the smaller, easier daily pit, and Breeo itself calls it a size for most spaces. If you host a larger ring of chairs and you regularly have 20-to-24-inch pieces, the X30 earns the extra diameter. Leave room to walk behind the chairs, not just room for the steel. I have not sat at either size in my yard, so I am not going to pretend I know which one “feels right.” I’m choosing between those two, on this pad, and I will buy one size, not both.

Corten or stainless, and what sits under it

Breeo sells the X24 and X30 in corten steel or 304 stainless. Their own note is that corten arrives in a blue steel finish and develops a reddish patina over time. Stainless stays closer to the color you bought. That is a looks-and-maintenance choice, not a performance ranking I can make from a desk.

Breeo also warns not to use the pits on heat-sensitive surfaces. A paver pad is the right idea. Do not set one on a wood deck or a composite surface and hope a mat is enough. If you add a lid, a base, or a spark screen, confirm the accessory is made for that exact size. I have not picked those add-ons yet.

A rack for the wood

A pit is only half the job if the wood sits in the mud. I looked at US-made racks from Woodhaven, and I narrowed it to the 8-foot Pioneer rack with a cover, with brown support bases if I can get them. I asked about a discount; I have not ordered it. Confirm the current length, cover, and finish on Woodhaven’s page. An 8-foot rack is a lot of steel — measure the wall or the side of the pad before you copy my shopping list.

  • Woodhaven Pioneer firewood rack with cover: Pioneer rack on Woodhaven’s site
  • Woodhaven support bases (confirm color and pack size): Woodhaven support bases

A cheaper rack from a big-box aisle can work if it is sturdy, covered, and kept off the soil. I would rather pay for a rack that will still be straight in five winters than save money on something that leans in one. I have not run a side-by-side rust test, so I will not name a “best budget rack.”

Things I would skip

  • A thin, no-name bowl on a wooden deck.
  • The largest pit in the catalog on a pad that also has to hold chairs and a walkway.
  • Treating “smokeless” as a promise that your neighbors will never smell a fire. Wind and wet wood still matter. Burn dry wood, and read the current user guide.
  • Storing wood against the house siding, where it can hold moisture and bugs.

Straight recommendation

On a paver pad about 12 to 14 feet across, choose a Breeo X24 or X30 by how many people you actually sit and how long a log you want to burn, then confirm the current price, finish, and accessories on Breeo’s site. Add a covered rack that fits the space you have — the Woodhaven Pioneer is the one I’m looking at, not one I can review after a season of weather. I will update this if I buy the pit and learn something a spec sheet cannot tell you.
