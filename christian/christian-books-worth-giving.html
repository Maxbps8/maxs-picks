Christian books worth giving — a short, plain list

This is a short list of Christian books that have been widely read for a long time, plus a few practical titles. It is not a statement that I have personally reread every page this year, and it is not a ranked “best Christian books of all time” claim. Books that have lasted

  • Mere Christianity — C.S. Lewis. Still the book many people hand to a thoughtful friend who wants the core of the faith explained in ordinary language. It is an argument, not a memoir. Bookshop.org · Amazon · Christianbook
  • The Screwtape Letters — C.S. Lewis. A short, sharp novel-in-letters about temptation. Easier to give than a systematic theology, and it still lands. Bookshop.org · Amazon · Christianbook
  • The Great Divorce — C.S. Lewis. A short imaginative picture of heaven and hell. Better for a reader who already likes Lewis than as a first introduction. Bookshop.org · Amazon · Christianbook
  • The Pilgrim’s Progress — John Bunyan. The classic allegory. Some modern-English editions are easier than others; check the edition, not only the title. Bookshop.org · Amazon · Christianbook
  • Knowing God — J.I. Packer. A serious, readable book about the character of God. A better gift for someone who wants depth than for a casual reader. Bookshop.org · Amazon · Christianbook
  • Confessions — Augustine (choose a good modern translation; Sarah Ruden or Henry Chadwick are often recommended — confirm the translator on the page). Old, personal, and still worth reading. Not a light gift. Bookshop.org · Amazon · Christianbook

Practical and pastoral

  • The Cost of Discipleship — Dietrich Bonhoeffer. Demanding, and it should be. Know the reader before you wrap it as a cheerful present. Bookshop.org · Amazon · Christianbook
  • A Praying Life — Paul E. Miller. A practical book on prayer that many ordinary readers have actually finished. Bookshop.org · Amazon · Christianbook
  • The Hiding Place — Corrie ten Boom. A wartime memoir of faith, hiding, and a concentration camp. Powerful, and not gentle. Bookshop.org · Amazon · Christianbook

How I would use this list

Pick one book that matches the reader, not a stack. Lewis is the usual safe starting point. Packer or Bonhoeffer is for someone who wants to be stretched. A memoir like The Hiding Place is for a reader who will sit with a hard story.

General history, business, mysteries, and other non-devotional reading lives in the main Books section of this site, not only here.
